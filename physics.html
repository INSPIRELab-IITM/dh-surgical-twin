Physics

Every soft organ below the diaphragm is a live deformable body in SOFA, on the GPU in double precision. Our code builds the scene, moves the instruments under their limits and reads the results; the forces are SOFA's.

Soft organs on the GPU

  • Corotational tetrahedra on a patched SofaCUDA in double precision: the liver, gallbladder, the Calot fat, the diaphragm, stomach, duodenum, small bowel, colon, spleen and kidney are live; the lungs, the abdominal wall and the training objects are replayed.
  • The hollow viscera's content as a Tait pressure term; the bile ducts and the cystic artery as bead chains with springs.
  • The live step adapts (1/60, 1/50, 1/40 or 0.03 s) to the finest the whole loop holds in real time, quality first.
  • Runs repeat to the bit with address randomisation off.

Contact and grasping

  • Constraint-based frictional contact with the instruments' drawn jaws and shafts; the shaft never enters an organ.
  • Grasping holds the tissue between the jaws; a bite tears only when its pull reaches the tissue's tear force, from the literature: liver 2.5 N, gallbladder 10 N, spleen 1.5 N, stomach 8 N, duodenum and small bowel 5 N, colon 4 N.
  • Blunt dissection of the fat; a duct or artery pulled past its limit is avulsed on its own axial tension.
  • Blunt objects never tear tissue.

Cutting, clips and sutures

  • A cut is computed in a worker process and eased open on screen, with no pop or jump; scalpel strokes are smoothed in tissue coordinates, so the walls are not jagged. A cut organ's constraint compliance follows its new mesh.
  • Robot scissors cut what is between their open blades.
  • Clips close a duct or an artery; dividing between clips is judged by the task.
  • Sutures hold: each stitch enters and leaves on the outer surface straight across the wound, a tied stitch closes the wound's depth near it, and a closed wound stops oozing.

Energy, smoke, bleeding and bile

  • The hook burns and coagulates where it touches; a guard can withhold the energy when a duct is too near.
  • Cautery smoke rises from the burn and is evacuated within seconds.
  • An incision oozes in proportion to its depth; a liver cut bleeds from the synthetic portal and hepatic vessels it crosses. Branches under 1 mm ooze and the hook seals them; larger ones bleed until sutured.
  • A perforated gallbladder leaks bile at a rate set by the hole; a bite shuts most of it, a clip or a stitch closes it.
  • The page draws the blood and bile the simulator loses as films that pool and drip.

Breathing

The abdomen breathes. Each scene carries its own respiration (built by the pipeline), so the targets move under the instruments: in the cholecystectomy, breathing moves the cystic duct by 9–13 mm, and a hook press follows the tissue it was picked on.
